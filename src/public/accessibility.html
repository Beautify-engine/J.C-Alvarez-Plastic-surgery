Accessibility

A lot of people read a site like this one-handed, at night, on a phone, worried. Some read it at 200% zoom, or with a screen reader, or without using a mouse at all. It should work for all of them.

Last updated 27 August 2026

The standard

This site targets WCAG 2.2 Level AA. That is a measurable bar, not a statement of intent, and the checks below are run against every page rather than a sample.

What has been tested

  • Automated checks on every page, at 1440px and 390px, using axe-core against the WCAG 2.0, 2.1 and 2.2 A and AA rule sets. Every page currently reports zero violations.
  • Keyboard only. Every control can be reached and operated without a mouse, including the results filters, the case viewer and the carousels. Focus is always visible.
  • Text over video. The homepage headline sits over moving footage, which automated tools cannot evaluate at all. The contrast was measured by sampling the actual composited pixels behind the text across the whole loop and taking the brightest frame as the worst case. It passes at that frame, not just on average.
  • Reduced motion. If your system asks for less movement, the hero video does not load, carousels do not animate, and transitions are removed.
  • 320px and 200% zoom. No horizontal scrolling, no clipped text.
  • Structure. One h1 per page, no skipped heading levels, real landmarks, and a skip link.

What is not good enough yet

Every site claiming to be accessible has gaps. These are ours.

  • The before-and-after images carry text inside the picture. The procedure name and the “Before” and “After” labels are part of the photograph rather than real text, so they cannot be resized or restyled. Each image has alt text carrying the same information, but that is a workaround.
  • The video talks are in Spanish with automatic captions only. They have not been captioned by a person in either language, and automatic captions of medical vocabulary are unreliable.
  • The hero video has no captions or audio description. It carries no speech and no information that is not repeated in the text beside it, but it has not been described.
  • Nothing here has been tested with real assistive technology by someone who depends on it. Automated checks and keyboard testing catch a great deal and are not the same thing.

If something does not work

Tell us and it will be fixed. Please say what page you were on and what you were using — that is usually enough to reproduce it.
