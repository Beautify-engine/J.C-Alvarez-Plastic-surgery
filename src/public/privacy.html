Privacy

This page describes what this website does with information. It is written to be read, not to be survived.

Last updated 27 August 2026

What the site collects

One thing: the consultation request form. If you fill it in and send it, it collects what you typed.

  • Your name and email address — required, because otherwise nobody can reply to you.
  • Your phone number — optional.
  • The procedure you are asking about, roughly when you are thinking of, and which language you would like to be contacted in.
  • Anything you write in the message box. Please do not put detailed medical history in it. A first message does not need one, and email is not a secure channel for it.

The form also contains one hidden field that is invisible to you and is left blank by every real person. Automated spam fills it in, which is how those submissions get discarded. It collects nothing about you.

What the site does not do

This is short, and deliberately so.

  • No cookies. The site sets none.
  • No analytics and no tracking pixels. There is no Google Analytics, no Meta pixel, no tag manager, and nothing that records what you looked at.
  • No advertising or remarketing of any kind. Reading a page about a procedure will not follow you around the internet.
  • The typefaces are served from this site, not from Google Fonts, so loading a page does not tell a font provider that you visited.

Embedded video and the map

Some pages show a video or a map. Nothing is requested from YouTube or Google until you click play. Until then you are looking at a still image served from this site. When you do click, that service receives the request and its own terms apply — YouTube is embedded through youtube-nocookie.com, which is its reduced-tracking option.

Links to Instagram, YouTube, Amazon and RealSelf leave this site. What those companies do is governed by their policies, not this one.

Photographs of patients

The before-and-after photographs on this site are Dr. Alvarez’s own patients, published with their consent. If you are one of them and you want an image removed, write to the address below and it will be taken down — you do not have to give a reason and you will not be asked for one.

Before this page goes live

The sections above describe what the website itself does, and each of those statements was checked against the code. The following cannot be answered from the code and are not guessed here. They need Dr. Alvarez and, for the last two, his attorney.

  1. Where a submitted form actually goes. The form currently has no destination wired. Once it posts to a CRM, an inbox or a scheduling system, that processor has to be named here.
  2. How long enquiries are kept, and who inside the practice can read them.
  3. Whether this practice is a HIPAA covered entity, and whether a website enquiry is treated as protected health information. This determines whether a general privacy notice is sufficient or whether a Notice of Privacy Practices is required.
  4. Which state privacy laws apply and what rights notice Florida requires.
  5. A contact for privacy requests. A shared Gmail address is probably not the right endpoint for a deletion request about medical enquiries.

This page was drafted from the site’s actual behaviour. It is not legal advice and it has not been reviewed by an attorney.
