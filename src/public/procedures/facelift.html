One operation, five moves.

Under general anaesthesia. The name describes a depth, not a degree — a deep plane facelift is not a bigger facelift, it is a different layer.

01 / 05

Or hear him explain it

  1. 01

    The incision is hidden

    Around the contour of the ear and back into the hairline, following lines that already exist on the face. Where it sits is the difference between a scar you look for and one you find.

  2. 02

    The deep plane is entered

    Below the SMAS — the muscular layer under the skin. Most facelifts work at or just under the skin. This one goes beneath the layer that actually holds the face down.

  3. 03

    The retaining ligaments are released

    The fibrous ligaments tethering the midface are divided. Nothing lifts convincingly until they are, which is why skin-only lifts pull rather than restore.

  4. 04

    Deep structures are repositioned

    The lift comes from underneath, moving the deep layer back to where it sat. The skin is then redraped over it rather than dragged.

  5. 05

    Closed without tension

    Because the lift is carried by the deep layer, the skin closes slack. Surface tension is what produces the pulled look, and there is none here to produce it.

People should notice you look rested. Not that you look operated on.

That is the entire test, and it is decided by which layer does the lifting. A skin-only lift borrows tension from the surface, and the surface is where the tell shows — the flat expression, the pulled mouth, the ear that has moved. Lift from underneath and the skin has nothing to fight.

A facelift does not treat your skin.

It moves what is underneath it. Lines, texture and pigment are a separate problem with separate answers — and expecting one operation to solve both is the most common reason someone is disappointed by a technically good result.

Five things he checks.

The same five he works through in the room. A surgeon who operates on everyone who asks is not selecting for results.

Sagging that starts below the skin

Likely a candidateJowls along the jawline, the midface dropping, a neck that has lost its angle. Structural descent is what this operation moves.

He’ll ask you to waitIf what bothers you is surface — fine lines, texture, pigment. That is resurfacing, and a facelift will not touch it.

You have seen results you do not want

Likely a candidateWanting undetectable is the right instinct, and it is what the deep plane technique is for.

He’ll ask you to waitIf you want an obvious, dramatic change. He is not the surgeon for that, and he will say so.

Two weeks you can actually take

Likely a candidateTen to fourteen days before most people are presentable, with bruising still fading after that.

He’ll ask you to waitIf you have an event in three weeks. Swelling does not negotiate, and residual swelling runs to three months.

Nicotine-free

Likely a candidateNot smoking, not vaping. Facial skin flaps depend entirely on their blood supply.

He’ll ask you to waitIf you are still smoking. This is the operation where nicotine causes the most visible harm — skin loss along the incision.

Done with temporary fixes

Likely a candidateLooking for something structural rather than another round of filler that adds volume without addressing descent.

He’ll ask you to waitIf you have not tried anything yet and want to start small. There are lighter options, and he will point at them.

Before you decide.

Surgery on the face, where every complication is visible. Every item below is a genuine possibility, not fine print.

  • Nerve injuryBranches of the facial nerve run through the field. Temporary weakness — an eyebrow that will not lift, a lopsided smile — is uncommon but real, and recovers over weeks to months. Permanent weakness is rare and serious.
  • HaematomaBleeding under the skin flap, most likely in the first 24 hours. It is the commonest early complication and needs treating promptly, not watching.
  • Permanent scarsAround the ear and into the hairline. Placed in existing contours and generally hard to find, but they do not go away.
  • NumbnessAcross the cheeks, ears and neck. Normal, and slow — months rather than weeks. Small patches can stay numb.
  • Skin healing problemsThe flap depends on its blood supply. This is the operation where smoking causes the most visible damage.
  • Hair loss near the incisionThinning along the hairline where the incision runs. Usually temporary.
  • AsymmetryTwo sides of a face were never identical and do not heal identically. Small differences are expected.

Still asked, often

Will I look “done”?

The pulled look comes from lifting the skin and taking tension at the surface. A deep plane lift moves the layer underneath and lets the skin sit back down slack, which is why it reads as rested rather than tight.

What is the difference from a SMAS facelift?

Depth. A SMAS lift tightens the muscular layer from above it. A deep plane lift works beneath that layer and releases the ligaments holding the midface down, so the lift is carried by structure rather than by skin.

Does it do anything for my neck?

Usually it is planned together — the jawline and neck angle are the first things people notice. Whether the neck needs separate work is decided at the consultation, and it changes the price.

Is a thread lift a lighter version of this?

No. Threads suspend tissue temporarily and do not release anything. They are a different intervention with a different lifespan, not a smaller facelift.

Will it fix my lines and skin texture?

No. A facelift repositions structure. Lines, texture and pigment are a surface problem and need resurfacing or skin treatment — often planned alongside, but they are not the same operation.

How long before I can be seen?

Ten to fourteen days for most people, with bruising still fading. Swelling settles substantially by six weeks and completely by around three months.
