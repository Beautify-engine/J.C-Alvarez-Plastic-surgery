  4. 04

  5. 05

    Self-dissolving sutures and tape, then a surgical bra to hold everything while the tissue settles onto its new position.

A lift trades a scar for a shape.

That is the whole bargain, and it is the part people are not told plainly. An augmentation hides its incision in a fold. A lift cannot — skin has to come out, and the scar runs around the areola and usually down from it. It fades substantially, it does not disappear, and if that trade is not worth it to you then an implant alone is the honest answer even if the result is lower.

Where your nipple sits decides which operation you need.

Not what you ask for, and not what a photograph shows. Above the crease beneath the breast, an implant alone can work. Below it, no implant will raise it — a bigger one just makes a heavier breast in the same place. He will measure it and show you, which takes about a minute.

Five things he checks.

The same five he works through in the room. A surgeon who operates on everyone who asks is not selecting for results.

A nipple that sits below the fold

Likely a candidateThe objective test. If the nipple has dropped below the crease beneath the breast, an implant alone will not raise it and a lift is the honest answer.

He’ll ask you to waitIf it still sits above the fold. An augmentation alone may do what you want, with a scar hidden in the crease.

Volume gone as well as position

Likely a candidateAfter pregnancy, breastfeeding or weight loss, the breast is often both lower and emptier. Lifting alone gives a perkier but still empty breast.

He’ll ask you to waitIf there is plenty of tissue and only position has changed. A lift on its own is the smaller operation, and he will say so.

The scar is a trade you accept

Likely a candidateAround the areola, usually with a line running down from it. It fades substantially over a year.

He’ll ask you to waitIf the scar is unacceptable to you. That is a legitimate answer, and the alternative is an implant alone with a lower result.

Finished having children

Likely a candidateOr certain that you are. Pregnancy and breastfeeding stretch both the skin envelope and the position again.

He’ll ask you to waitIf a pregnancy is close. Everything this operation corrects can be undone by one.

Nicotine-free

Likely a candidateNot smoking, not vaping. Lifting a breast means moving tissue on its blood supply, and doing that alongside an implant raises the stakes.

He’ll ask you to waitIf you are still smoking. This is the breast operation where nicotine causes the most visible harm, including at the nipple itself.

Before you decide.

Two operations at once carries both sets of risks, and one that belongs to neither alone.

Doing both at once is a real trade-off.

A lift tightens the skin envelope; an implant pushes outward against it. The two work against each other, which is why some surgeons stage them — lift first, implant months later — and why doing both in one sitting carries a higher revision rate than either alone. Ask him which he recommends for you and why, rather than assuming one sitting is simply more efficient.
  • A permanent scarAround the areola, usually with a vertical line beneath and sometimes a horizontal one. It fades substantially over a year. It does not go away.
  • Loss of nipple sensationHigher than with an augmentation alone, because the nipple is moved on its blood and nerve supply. Usually partial and temporary; occasionally permanent.
  • Nipple healing problemsRare and serious. Moving the nipple depends on its blood supply, which is exactly what smoking compromises.
  • Capsular contractureScar tissue tightening around the implant, making the breast firm or distorted. The commonest reason a good result needs revising, sometimes years later.
  • Implant ruptureDevices fail eventually. Silicone rupture is often silent, which is why periodic imaging is advised.
  • Recurrent droopingA lift resets position; it does not stop gravity or time. An implant adds weight, which does not help.
  • Effects on breastfeedingMore likely to be affected than by an augmentation alone, because the tissue around the nipple is moved. It cannot be guaranteed either way.

What it costs

Every quote is built from the same things. Ask whether the lift and the implant are quoted together or separately — and ask what a revision would cost, because this combination needs them more often.

  • Which lift pattern is needed — the scar length drives the work
  • Implant type and profile
  • Whether it is being staged or done in one sitting
  • Operating time and anaesthesia
  • Facility fee, garments and follow-up
  • What a revision would cost, and who pays for it

Still asked, often

Do I need the implant as well, or just the lift?

A lift alone repositions what you have and will look smaller on top, because nothing has been added. If you also want fullness in the upper pole, that is what the implant is for. He will show you both on your own measurements.

How long is the scar?

It depends on how much skin has to come out. Around the areola alone for a small correction; with a vertical line below it for most; with a horizontal line as well where there is a lot. More skin removed means more scar and a better shape — that is the trade.

Should it be done in one operation or two?

Both are legitimate. One sitting is one recovery and one cost; staging gives each part a cleaner result and is sometimes safer where the lift is large. It is a real conversation, not a default.

Will it droop again?

Eventually, to some extent. The operation resets position; it does not stop time, and an implant adds weight. Stable weight and good support slow it.

Can I still breastfeed?

Possibly, but less predictably than after an augmentation alone, because the tissue around the nipple is moved. If it matters, say so at the consultation — it influences the technique.

Why is my nipple position marked while I am standing?

Because that is where gravity holds it. Marked lying down, a nipple ends up too high once you stand — and a nipple set too high is the one error in this operation that cannot be corrected afterwards.
