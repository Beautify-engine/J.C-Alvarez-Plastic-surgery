One operation, six moves.

Ninety minutes to two and a half hours under general anaesthesia. Three separate problems — loose skin, a separated muscle wall, fat that will not shift — and only one of them responds to training.

01 / 06

Or hear him explain it

  1. 01

    A low incision

    Along the bikini line, low enough to sit under underwear. Its length depends on how much skin is coming out.

  2. 02

    The loose skin comes out

    Pregnancy and weight loss stretch skin past its elasticity and it does not retract. The apron below the navel is removed with the fat attached to it.

  3. 03

    The muscle wall is closed

    Diastasis recti — the two vertical muscles have pulled apart and stay apart. Sutures draw them back together. This is what flattens the profile, and what no non-surgical treatment reproduces.

  4. 04

    The waist is shaped

    Where fat persists at a stable weight, liposuction defines the flanks so the new abdomen reads as part of the same body. Capped at one litre.

  5. 05

    The navel is reset

    Brought through the repositioned skin and reshaped. Making it look untouched is most of the craft in the operation.

  6. 06

    Closed, drained, compressed

    Drains stop fluid collecting, the incision closes in layers, and a binder goes on before you wake up.

Liposuction alongside a tummy tuck is capped at one litre by Florida law. He will not go around it.

About 2.2 pounds. Removing more in one sitting raises the risk of significant blood loss and fluid shifts. Want more than that? Then it is two operations, spaced apart — and any surgeon offering to do it all at once is telling you something about how they practise.

A tummy tuck is not a weight-loss operation.

It is for people already close to the weight they mean to keep. Planning to lose more? Then it waits — otherwise you are paying to remove skin you are about to replace.

Five things he checks.

The same five he works through in the room. A surgeon who operates on everyone who asks is not selecting for results.

Loose skin that no longer retracts

Likely a candidateAfter pregnancy or significant weight loss, skin stretched past its elasticity does not come back on its own.

He’ll ask you to waitIf you are still planning to lose a meaningful amount of weight. You would be removing skin you are about to replace.

A separation you can feel

Likely a candidateA ridge or bulge down the midline when you sit up. That is the muscle wall, and sutures are the only thing that closes it.

He’ll ask you to waitIf what you can pinch is fat rather than a gap, liposuction alone may be the smaller operation you actually need.

A weight you can hold

Likely a candidateClose to the weight you intend to keep. The result lasts exactly as long as that does.

He’ll ask you to waitIf your weight has moved a lot in the last year. He would rather operate once, later, than twice.

Finished having children

Likely a candidateOr certain that you are. The repair is permanent as long as nothing stretches it again.

He’ll ask you to waitIf a pregnancy is still possible. It stretches skin and muscle again and can undo the muscle repair entirely.

Nicotine-free

Likely a candidateNot smoking, not vaping, not patches. Disqualifying rather than discouraged.

He’ll ask you to waitIf you are still smoking. Nicotine constricts the blood supply exactly where the incision needs it most.

Before you decide.

Real surgery on the abdominal wall. Every item below is a genuine possibility, not fine print.

  • NumbnessNormal across the abdomen, improving over weeks to months. In some areas it can be permanent.
  • A permanent scarLow and placed under clothing, but long. It fades rather than disappears.
  • Fluid collection (seroma)Fluid gathering under the skin. Drains are placed to manage it.
  • Bleeding and infectionManaged with technique and monitoring, not eliminated.
  • Deep vein thrombosisA clot in the leg. Why walking starts on day one and smoking is disqualifying.
  • An outcome that needs revisionSome results need a second, smaller procedure. Ask what that costs before you book, not after.

What it costs

Every quote is built from the same six things. Ask for them itemised — a single number tells you nothing about what is in it.

  • Full abdominoplasty or mini
  • Whether the muscle repair is needed
  • Whether liposuction is added, and to how many areas
  • Operating time and anaesthesia
  • Facility fee
  • Garments, drains and the follow-up schedule

Still asked, often

Full or mini — which one applies to me?

A full abdominoplasty treats the whole abdomen and repairs the muscle. A mini works only below the navel, through a shorter incision, with a shorter recovery. Where your loose skin actually sits decides it, not which sounds easier.

Will it remove my stretch marks?

The ones on the skin being removed — typically below the navel. Marks above the navel or out on the flanks stay where they are.

Will my belly button look different?

It is repositioned and reshaped as part of the operation. Done well it looks like a navel that was always there.

Can I have children afterwards?

Yes, but pregnancy stretches the skin and the muscle repair again and can undo the result. The recommendation is to wait until after your last pregnancy.

Will it help my back pain or posture?

Where back pain comes from a separated abdominal wall, restoring the core can reduce it. A documented secondary benefit, not a reason to have surgery.

Is it painful?

The muscle repair is the part that hurts, worst in the first several days. Pain management is planned before the operation rather than improvised after it.
