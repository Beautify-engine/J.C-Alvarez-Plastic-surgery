Or hear him explain it

  1. 01

    The frame is measured first

    On a lean patient there is no volume to hide behind, so the plan is drawn from your proportions — waist, hip width, the height of the fold beneath the buttock. Getting that wrong shows immediately.

  2. 02

    Every donor site is harvested

    Not one area but several — abdomen, flanks, back, inner thighs, the fat pad above the knee. On a lean frame the yield from any single site is small, so the harvest is wider and gentler.

  3. 03

    Fat is purified carefully

    With less to work with, how much survives matters far more than it does on a large transfer. The processing is the same; the margin for waste is not.

  4. 04

    It is placed for shape, not size

    Small volumes, precisely positioned above the muscle — filling a hip dip, lifting the upper pole, closing the gap between thigh and buttock. The change is in outline rather than mass.

  5. 05

    Compression, and his recovery protocol

    A faja immediately. He also runs hyperbaric oxygen and regenerative adjuncts as part of aftercare — his own long-standing interest, and the reason recovery is a section of this practice rather than a leaflet.

You were told you did not have enough fat.

That is usually why people arrive here, and it is often true of a conventional BBL — there is not enough to add real volume. It is not the same as having nothing to work with. On a lean frame a small, precisely placed transfer changes an outline rather than a size, and for a lot of people that was what they wanted in the first place.

Less fat means less margin, not less operation.

A small transfer is not a smaller procedure. The harvest is wider, the processing matters more, and a graft this size has almost no tolerance for a shortcut in recovery. The compression and the sitting restriction are identical to a full BBL.

Five things he checks.

The same five he works through in the room. A surgeon who operates on everyone who asks is not selecting for results.

Lean, and staying that way

Likely a candidateA low body fat percentage you hold steady. Transferred fat behaves like the fat it came from, and on a lean frame there is no reserve to lose.

He’ll ask you to waitIf your weight moves. Losing even a little afterwards takes a small graft with it, and there is less margin here than on a full transfer.

Outline, not volume

Likely a candidateWanting a hip dip filled, an upper pole lifted, a silhouette closed up. Changes measured in shape rather than size.

He’ll ask you to waitIf you want obvious volume. On this frame there is not enough fat to deliver it, and stretching for it is how results go wrong.

Some fat somewhere

Likely a candidateEven lean bodies hold pockets — flanks, inner thigh, above the knee, the lower back. Several small sites together make a transfer possible.

He’ll ask you to waitIf there is genuinely nothing to harvest. He will say so rather than take a deposit, and an implant is a different conversation.

Three months of compression, and no sitting on it

Likely a candidateThe same recovery as a full BBL. Less fat does not mean less protecting it — if anything a small graft is more vulnerable.

He’ll ask you to waitIf you cannot take the pressure off for the first weeks. This is the part people underestimate and the part that decides the result.

Nicotine-free

Likely a candidateNot smoking, not vaping. Transferred fat needs a blood supply to survive, and a small graft has less to spare.

He’ll ask you to waitIf you are still smoking. More of the graft dies, and on this volume that is the whole result.

Before you decide.

The risks are a fat transfer’s risks. The one that differs is how little room there is for the graft to fail.

Ask where the fat goes.

The safety of any gluteal fat transfer turns on one thing: fat placed in the layer above the muscle, never into it. Injection into the gluteal muscle caused the deaths that gave this operation its reputation. It is the first question to put to any surgeon offering you one, at any volume.
  • Fat embolismThe specific risk of a gluteal transfer, whatever the volume. Rare, serious, and the reason placement above the muscle is not a stylistic preference.
  • Losing more of the graft than expectedEvery transfer reabsorbs some. On a small one there is little to spare, so the same percentage loss is a bigger proportion of your result.
  • Contour irregularity at the donor siteHarvesting small amounts from several lean areas is more technically demanding than taking a lot from one. Unevenness shows more on a lean body, not less.
  • AsymmetryTwo sides rarely absorb identically, and at low volumes a small difference is a visible one.
  • Needing a second transferSometimes the honest answer after one round is that a second, small session would finish it. Worth asking what that would cost before you start.
  • Deep vein thrombosisA clot in the leg. Why walking starts on day one and why smoking is disqualifying.

What it costs

Every quote is built from the same things. On this operation, ask particularly about the liposuction — harvesting from several small sites is most of the work.

  • How many donor areas are harvested
  • Whether a lipo 360 is included or extra
  • Operating time and anaesthesia
  • Facility fee
  • Garments, and how many
  • Whether a second session is likely, and what it would cost

Still asked, often

How is this different from a normal BBL?

It is the same operation with a smaller transfer, for people who do not have the fat for a large one. The difference is in what it can achieve — a change of outline rather than added volume.

I was told I am not a candidate. Why is this different?

Being turned down for a conventional BBL usually means there is not enough fat to add real volume. That is a different question from whether there is enough to reshape. He assesses the donor sites rather than the number.

Would an implant be better for me?

Sometimes. Implants add volume regardless of how lean you are, but they bring displacement, infection and revision risks that fat does not, and they do not shape the waist the way harvesting does. It is a real conversation, not a default.

Will I need a second round?

Possibly. On small transfers a staged second session is a legitimate plan rather than a failure, and it is better discussed at the start than offered afterwards.

Is the recovery easier because it is smaller?

No. Three months of compression and the same sitting restriction. A small graft is if anything more vulnerable to being sat on.

Do the hyperbaric and regenerative treatments help?

They are part of his aftercare protocol and reflect a long-standing interest of his in healing quality. How much they improve graft survival is still being established — ask him what he expects them to do for you specifically.
