01 / 05

Or hear him explain it

  1. 01

    Marked while you are sitting up

    Skin is measured and marked before anything is given, with you upright — because that is how gravity will hold your eyelid for the rest of your life. Symmetry is decided here, not in theatre.

  2. 02

    Local or general, depending

    Upper lids alone can be done under local anaesthesia. Both lids, or lower lids with fat repositioning, usually warrant general.

  3. 03

    Skin removed, fat repositioned

    Excess upper lid skin is excised. On the lower lid the modern move is to reposition fat into the hollow beneath rather than simply remove it — taking too much is what produces the sunken, skeletal look.

  4. 04

    Closed with very fine sutures

    In the natural crease of the upper lid, or just under the lash line below. Both hide almost completely once settled.

  5. 05

    Elevated, cooled, protected

    Head up, cold compresses, drops, and sunglasses for a fortnight. Most of what determines the first week is aftercare rather than surgery.

It fixes tired. It does not fix dark circles.

Blepharoplasty removes excess skin and deals with the fat that makes lids puffy. It does nothing for pigment under the eye, for crow’s feet, or for the fine crepe texture of the skin itself — those are surface problems with surface answers. Booking surgery for them is how someone ends up disappointed by an operation that went perfectly.

Check the brow before you blame the lid.

A heavy brow pushes skin down onto the eyelid and looks exactly like excess lid skin. Remove the lid skin and the brow simply drops further. Which one is actually causing it is the first thing he assesses, and getting it wrong is the classic way this operation disappoints.

Five things he checks.

The same five he works through in the room. A surgeon who operates on everyone who asks is not selecting for results.

A lid that hangs

Likely a candidateExcess upper lid skin resting on the lashes, or heavy enough to narrow your field of vision.

He’ll ask you to waitIf the heaviness is coming from the brow rather than the lid. Lifting the wrong structure makes the eye look worse, not better.

Bags that are fat, not fluid

Likely a candidateLower lid puffiness that is there every morning regardless of sleep or salt.

He’ll ask you to waitIf it changes hour to hour. That is fluid, and surgery is the wrong tool for it.

Skin with some elasticity left

Likely a candidateEnough tone that the lid settles cleanly once skin is removed.

He’ll ask you to waitIf the lower lid is lax enough to pull away from the eye. That needs tightening at the same time, or the lid can round or droop afterwards.

A fortnight you can plan around

Likely a candidateSeven to ten days before most people are presentable, with bruising still fading after that.

He’ll ask you to waitIf you have an event in three weeks. Eyelid bruising is conspicuous and it does not hurry.

Healthy eyes, nicotine-free

Likely a candidateNo untreated dry eye or thyroid eye disease, and not smoking.

He’ll ask you to waitIf your eyes are already dry. This operation can make dry eye worse, and it needs assessing first rather than discovering afterwards.

Before you decide.

Surgery millimetres from the eye. Small operation, unforgiving margins — every item below is a genuine possibility, not fine print.

  • Dry eyeCommon for weeks afterwards, and it can be prolonged. If you already have dry eye, this operation can worsen it, which is why it is assessed before booking.
  • Difficulty closing the eye fullyUsually temporary while swelling settles. Persistent trouble closing means too much skin was taken, and it is the reason conservative removal beats generous.
  • A lower lid that pulls down or roundsEctropion. More likely where lid laxity was not addressed at the same time, and it can need a second operation.
  • AsymmetryTwo eyelids were never identical. Small differences are expected; noticeable ones may need adjusting.
  • Visible scarsPlaced in the upper crease and beneath the lash line, where they hide almost completely — but they are permanent, and they pigment if exposed to sun early.
  • Bleeding behind the eyeRare and a genuine emergency, because pressure behind the globe can threaten vision. It is why you are given specific instructions about what to do if pain and swelling escalate suddenly.
  • A hollow, skeletal lookCaused by removing too much lower lid fat. Repositioning it rather than excising it is what avoids this, and it is the difference between looking rested and looking gaunt.

What it costs

Every quote is built from the same things. Ask for them itemised — upper and lower lids are separate operations that happen to be booked together.

  • Upper lids, lower lids, or both
  • Whether lower lid tightening is needed as well
  • Local or general anaesthesia
  • Operating time and facility fee
  • The follow-up schedule
  • Whether a brow lift is being considered alongside

Still asked, often

Will it get rid of my dark circles?

No. Dark circles are usually pigment or shadow from lost volume. Surgery removes skin and moves fat; it does nothing to pigment. Sometimes correcting the hollow reduces the shadow, but do not book for that alone.

Is it my eyelids or my brow?

Frequently the brow. A heavy brow pushes skin onto the lid and looks identical to excess lid skin. He checks by lifting the brow into position and showing you what changes.

Upper, lower, or both?

Upper lids address hooding and heaviness. Lower lids address bags. They are different operations with different recoveries and can be done together or staged.

Will it look obvious?

Done conservatively, no — people tend to say you look rested. The obvious result comes from taking too much skin or too much fat, which is why the marking is done sitting upright and deliberately cautiously.

How long does it last?

Upper lid surgery generally lasts many years. Ageing continues, so it slows the clock rather than stopping it.

Can it be done under local anaesthetic?

Upper lids alone often can. Lower lids, or both together, usually warrant a general.
