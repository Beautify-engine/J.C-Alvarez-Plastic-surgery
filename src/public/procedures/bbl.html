One operation, five moves.

Under general anaesthesia. Two operations really — a liposuction and a transfer — and the first one shapes the result as much as the second.

01 / 05

Or hear him explain it

  1. 01

    The fat is harvested

    Liposuction from the abdomen, flanks, back or thighs — wherever it is unwanted. The contouring this creates is half the result; the buttock is the other half.

  2. 02

    It is purified

    The harvested fat is processed to remove fluid and debris. Only viable fat cells are kept, because only viable cells survive the transfer.

  3. 03

    It is placed above the muscle

    Injected in fine passes into the subcutaneous layer. Never into the muscle — that distinction is the entire safety story of this operation.

  4. 04

    Proportion is set, not volume

    Where the waist and flanks are taken from decides the silhouette as much as what goes in. Between 300 and 1,000cc per side, judged against your frame rather than a target.

  5. 05

    Compression goes on

    A faja, immediately, to control swelling and hold the new contour while the transferred fat establishes a blood supply.

He will not combine this with a tummy tuck.

His own position, and an unusual one to publish — combining the two means a longer operation, more surfaces healing at once, and a patient who cannot lie on their front or their back. Plenty of surgeons will book it as one sitting. He asks you to stage it instead.

It is not about volume. It is about proportion.

The misconception is that a BBL means an exaggerated result. What actually decides whether it looks like anything is where the fat is taken from — the waist, the flanks, the back. Volume without contour is the look people are afraid of.

Five things he checks.

The same five he works through in the room. A surgeon who operates on everyone who asks is not selecting for results.

Enough fat to move

Likely a candidateThe operation needs a donor site. Abdomen, flanks, back or thighs — somewhere with fat worth harvesting.

He’ll ask you to waitIf you are very lean there may not be enough to transfer. That is what a skinny BBL is for, and the result is a lift rather than volume.

A shape in mind, not a size

Likely a candidateProportion against your own frame. The waist he takes from matters as much as the volume he puts in.

He’ll ask you to waitIf you arrive with a number of cc in mind, or a photograph of someone else. He will talk about your frame instead.

A weight you can hold

Likely a candidateTransferred fat behaves like the fat it came from. Stable weight keeps the result stable.

He’ll ask you to waitIf your weight swings. Losing significant weight afterwards takes the result with it.

Somewhere to recover

Likely a candidateThree months of compression and weeks of not sitting normally. It needs planning around work and family.

He’ll ask you to waitIf you cannot take the pressure off for the first weeks. This recovery is more disruptive than most people expect.

Nicotine-free

Likely a candidateNot smoking, not vaping, not patches. Transferred fat needs a blood supply to survive.

He’ll ask you to waitIf you are still smoking. Nicotine constricts exactly the vessels the graft depends on, and more of it dies.

Before you decide.

This procedure earned a reputation, and it is worth understanding exactly where that came from.

Ask where the fat goes.

The safety of this operation turns on one thing: fat placed in the layer above the muscle, never into it. Injection into the gluteal muscle is what caused the deaths that gave the BBL its reputation, and it is why the technique changed. It is the first question to put to any surgeon offering you one, and you should expect a straight answer.
  • Fat embolismThe specific risk of this operation: fat entering the bloodstream through a vein. Rare, serious, and the reason placement above the muscle is not a stylistic preference.
  • Not all of the fat survivesA proportion of every graft is reabsorbed in the first months. It is expected and planned for, not a complication.
  • An asymmetric resultTwo sides rarely absorb identically. Small differences are common; large ones may need a second, smaller transfer.
  • Contour irregularity at the donor siteThe liposuction half carries its own risk of unevenness, and it is the half people forget to ask about.
  • Fluid collection and infectionManaged with drains, compression and monitoring rather than eliminated.
  • Deep vein thrombosisA clot in the leg. Why walking starts on day one and why smoking is disqualifying.

What it costs

Every quote is built from the same things. Ask for them itemised — with this operation especially, a single number hides how much liposuction is included.

  • How many areas are liposuctioned to harvest
  • Whether a lipo 360 is included or extra
  • Operating time and anaesthesia
  • Facility fee
  • Garments, and how many
  • The follow-up and massage schedule

Still asked, often

How much fat is transferred?

Typically between 300 and 1,000cc per side. The number is decided by your frame and by how much viable fat the harvest yields — not by a target you bring in.

What is a skinny BBL?

The same operation with a smaller transfer, for patients with less fat to harvest. The result is a lift and a change of shape rather than added volume.

Can I combine it with other procedures?

With a breast augmentation or lift, commonly. Not with a tummy tuck — that is his own position, and he will explain why rather than book it.

Why can’t I sit normally?

Pressure on freshly transferred fat cuts off the blood supply it needs to survive. Sitting on it early is the most reliable way to lose the result you paid for. A BBL pillow keeps the weight on your thighs instead.

Is an implant safer than fat?

Different, not safer. Implants avoid the embolism risk but bring their own — displacement, infection and a foreign body that eventually needs attention. Fat is your own tissue and behaves like it.

How long does it last?

The fat that survives the first few months is permanent, in the sense that it is now living tissue in a new place. It gains and loses with the rest of you.
