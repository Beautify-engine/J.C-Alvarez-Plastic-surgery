Same word, two operations.

“Eyelid surgery” covers both of these. They differ in what they cut, what they can fix, and what they leave behind — and the smaller one is not simply a lighter version of the larger one.

The two lower-eyelid operations he offers, compared on the five things that decide between them.
The question Blepharoplasty Scarless · CO2
Where the incision goes Outside, in the crease below the lash line Inside the lower lid, against the eyeball
What happens to the skin Cut, and the excess removed Not cut. A CO2 laser tightens it from the surface
What it is suited to Advanced laxity, hooding, vision obstruction Mild to moderate bags and early skin change
Usually combined with A brow lift or a facelift Fillers, PRP, or a second laser pass later
What you are left with A fine external scar in the crease No external scar, and no stitches to remove

The smaller operation has a ceiling.

It takes fat out and tightens skin; it does not remove skin. So if what you have is genuine excess — skin that folds, or a lid that hangs — this will improve it slightly and disappoint you. That is the one thing worth establishing at the consultation before anything else, and it takes him about a minute to tell you which column you are in.

Forty-five minutes, four moves.

Local anaesthetic with mild sedation. You are not put under, and you go home the same day.

Or hear him explain it

  1. 01

    Numbed, and lightly sedated

    Local anaesthetic around the lower lid with mild sedation — not general anaesthesia. You will be drowsy and comfortable rather than unconscious, which is most of why the recovery is short.

  2. 02

    The incision is made inside the lid

    On the conjunctiva — the wet surface against the eyeball. It is a real incision, in a place that never becomes a visible scar and needs no stitches to remove.

  3. 03

    The fat pads are removed — or moved

    The bulge under your eye is orbital fat pushing forward. Some of it comes out; some of it is repositioned into the hollow below, which is often the better answer and the one that avoids a scooped, over-done look.

  4. 04

    The laser does the skin, from the outside

    A fractional CO2 laser resurfaces the lower lid without cutting it, so the skin tightens as it rebuilds collagen. Nothing is stitched. This half is what you actually spend the week recovering from.

The laser is the half people underestimate.

Taking the fat out is quick, and by the second day you have largely forgotten about it. The resurfacing is the part that decides your week: redness for three to seven days, then skin that keeps tightening for about three months as it rebuilds collagen. When someone says the recovery was easy, or that it was harder than they expected, they are almost always talking about the laser.

He is checking whether you need the other operation.

Almost everyone who asks about this one wants it to be the answer — it is shorter, cheaper, awake, and leaves nothing to see. So the consultation is mostly him testing whether it will actually do enough for you. Being told no here is the version of this appointment that saves you money.

Five things he checks.

His own list, and the reason he gives for waiting. Four of the five are about how much there is to fix, because that is the whole question here.

Bags rather than folds

Likely a candidatePuffiness under the eye that is fat pushing forward, mild to moderate. That is exactly what the transconjunctival approach reaches, and taking it out is the predictable half of this operation.

He’ll send you the other wayIf the bulge is severe, or loose skin hangs over it. Fat removal alone leaves an emptier version of the same shape.

Early skin change, not real excess

Likely a candidateCrepey texture and fine lines. The laser resurfaces that well, because it is tightening skin rather than removing it.

He’ll send you the other wayIf there is genuine excess skin, or the lid itself is lax. No amount of laser removes skin, and a blepharoplasty is the operation that does.

You want nothing visible

Likely a candidateThe reason most people choose this one. No external incision, no stitches to remove, and nothing about your face that announces an operation happened.

He’ll send you the other wayRarely — but he will say plainly if wanting nothing visible is pushing you toward an operation that will not do enough.

Expectations that match the ceiling

Likely a candidateYou want to look rested rather than different. That is what this delivers, and it is a good outcome rather than a consolation.

He’ll send you the other wayIf the photograph you brought is of a facelift. He would rather show you the gap now than have you find it at three months.

Willing to look after the skin afterwards

Likely a candidateSunscreen, and a routine you will actually keep. The laser result is skin quality, and skin quality is maintained or lost.

He’ll send you the other wayIf sun protection is not going to happen. In Miami that is not a small caveat, and it is the fastest way to undo what the laser did.

Before you decide.

A short list, because it is a small operation. Most of what can disappoint you here is not a complication — it is having chosen the wrong one of the two.

Ask what the laser does to your skin.

Resurfacing does not behave the same way on every complexion, and this is a Miami practice — his patients span a wide range. It is a fair and specific question: what has he seen on skin like yours, what settings does he change, and what does he do before and after to protect the result. A surgeon who does a lot of this will have an immediate answer.
  • It cannot remove skinThe most likely way to be disappointed, and it is a limit rather than a complication. If what you have is real excess, this improves it a little. Establish which column you are in before anything else.
  • Swelling and rednessThree to seven days for most people, and it comes from the laser rather than the incision. Covered with concealer after about a week.
  • Dryness and irritationThe eye is drier than usual while the surface heals. Managed with drops, and temporary.
  • AsymmetryTwo eyes are never identical to begin with. Fat is removed by judgement, not by measurement, and small differences can remain.
  • Taking out too muchAn over-emptied lower lid reads hollow and older, and it is harder to correct than the bulge was. It is the argument for repositioning fat rather than simply removing it.
  • Lid malpositionThe lower lid pulling down or away from the eye. Rare in general and rarer through this approach than through an external incision, because the muscle and skin at the lid margin are left alone.
  • How your skin responds to the laserNot every complexion resurfaces the same way. This is a question to put to him directly at the consultation rather than something to read off a page.

What it costs

This is the least expensive operation on the site, and the one most often quoted as a package. Ask what the package contains — the laser is the part that varies, and a second pass is a separate conversation.

  • Lower lids alone, or upper lids in the same sitting
  • How much laser resurfacing is planned, and at what depth
  • Local anaesthetic with sedation rather than general — usually the cheaper half
  • Facility fee and follow-up visits
  • Whether a second laser pass is included, or billed again

Still asked, often

Is it really scarless?

There is no scar you or anyone else can see. The incision is made on the inside of the lower lid, against the eyeball, and it heals without stitches. Calling it scarless is a claim about the outside of your face — which is the part that was ever the concern.

Does it remove any skin?

No. That is the defining difference from a blepharoplasty. The CO2 laser tightens and resurfaces the skin from the surface instead, which works well on texture and fine lines and does nothing for genuine excess.

Am I asleep for it?

No. It is done under local anaesthetic with mild sedation — drowsy and comfortable rather than unconscious — and takes roughly forty-five minutes to an hour. You go home the same day.

When can I go back to work?

Most people are back at a desk within five to seven days. Redness is the limiting factor rather than pain, and it takes concealer well after about a week.

How long does it last?

His published figure is five to ten years, depending on skin quality, sun exposure and how you age. The fat that was removed does not come back; the skin keeps ageing, which is the part sun protection actually affects.

Can it be combined with anything else?

Yes, and it often is — upper eyelids in the same sitting, or PRP, fillers and hyperbaric oxygen alongside it to support healing and skin quality. What is worth combining is a conversation about your face rather than a menu.
