Preparation & recovery

Healing is part of the operation.

Most of what decides your result happens when you are not in the operating room — in the weeks before your date, and the months after it.

Before he was a plastic surgeon, Dr. Alvarez spent years in research laboratories at Harvard University and the University of Pittsburgh studying stem cells, cell culturing and tissue repair. That is an unusual thing for a surgeon to have done, and it is why the plan you are given starts long before your date and does not stop at your last stitch.

Zero pain and zero inflammation don’t exist in a real body.

What he brings to it

A doctorate’s worth of interest in how tissue repairs.

Hyperbaric oxygen therapy is part of his practice — he trained in it and talks about it at length on his own channel. So is the regenerative side of the field he researched: exosomes, peptides, stem cells, and what each of them actually does.

None of it is a shortcut and he does not sell it as one. It is the same argument as the rest of this page: the operation is the part everyone photographs, and the healing is the part that decides what you keep.

What he will not tell you

That it will be easy.

  • Your plan is yours

    Recovery is not one timeline. It depends on the procedure, on whether you had more than one, on your age, your weight, whether you smoke, and how your tissue behaves. You get a plan built for your case at your consultation, in writing, before you commit to anything.

  • The first week is the hard one

    Every patient who mentions it says the same thing, and so does he. What he does not do is soften it so the decision is easier.

  • Ask him what can go wrong

    He will answer, in detail, including the outcomes that were not perfect and what he would do differently now. It is a strange thing to look forward to in a consultation. It is also the reason to book one.

Nothing on this page is medical advice or a set of post-operative instructions. Your instructions come from Dr. Alvarez, in person, for your procedure — and those are the ones to follow.
