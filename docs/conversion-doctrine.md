# Conversion doctrine

Read before designing or writing **any** section. Every section gets a stated job before
it gets a layout. If it doesn't have one, it doesn't ship.

---

## Who is actually on this page

A woman, 28–55, on an iPhone, at night. She has wanted this for **months or years** — the
desire is not something we create, it already exists. What we do is convert latent desire
into an inquiry.

She has already seen his Instagram. She is now comparing **2–4 surgeons**. She is not
deciding *whether* to have surgery. She is deciding *who she trusts to cut her*.

## The four fears that stop her booking

Every one of these is a section's job to answer. In order of how often they kill the sale:

| # | fear | what answers it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **"Will I look done / fake / obviously operated on?"** | Results that look natural, shown at volume. His own language about proportion and balance. |
| 2 | **"Will I actually get that result — or does it only work on girls who already look like that?"** | Cases of people who look like *her*. Filterable, varied, honest. |
| 3 | **"Is this safe? Could something go wrong?"** | Board certification, accredited facility, his research background, the recovery protocol. |
| 4 | **"What is recovery really like? Can I afford the downtime?"** | The 90-day preparation content, hyperbaric therapy, an honest timeline. |

## The strategic opening — and it is his, not ours

**Almost every plastic surgery site sells the result. Almost none sells the recovery.**

His own words: *"improve the quality of your healing after cosmetic surgery."* That is
fear #4 and fear #3, answered by a Ph.D. with a hyperbaric chamber and 90 days of
preparation content no competitor has written.

It is also a **desire** play, not just a trust play: less downtime and better healing makes
the surgery feel *attainable* — she can picture fitting it into her actual life. That moves
her from "someday" to "let me ask."

**This is the position. Everything else is support.**

## What actually converts here

- **Specificity beats superlatives.** "68 documented cases" outperforms "best in Miami," and
  it is the only one of the two we are allowed to say (§3).
- **Naming the fear out loud builds more trust than avoiding it.** A section titled *"Will
  it look done?"* converts better than one titled *"Our Philosophy."*
- **Self-identification is the whole game in the gallery.** She is not admiring results, she
  is searching for her own body. Filtering is a conversion feature, not a UI nicety.
- **The consult must feel like information, not commitment.** "Request a consultation"
  should read as *a conversation*, never as *a decision*.
- **Reduce unknowns.** Publishing what happens at the consult, and roughly what things cost,
  removes the two biggest reasons people stall.
- **The surgeon's face and voice.** She is choosing a person (§5.3).

## What we will not do

Barred by CLAUDE.md and by the fact that they cheapen a premium brand:

Fake scarcity or countdowns · "Only 3 spots left" · exit-intent popups · gating results
behind a form (§5.6) · superlatives or outcome guarantees (§3) · before/afters implying
typical results without context · fabricated testimonials or review counts · pressure
language in the CTA.

**In the premium segment restraint outperforms pressure.** A nervous 45-year-old spending
$12,000 is repelled by discount urgency — it reads as a mill. Calm, specific and evidenced
is the higher-converting register *and* the compliant one.

## Section job framework

Every section is assigned one primary job:

| job | what it must do |
|---|---|
| **TRUST** | make her believe he is safe and skilled |
| **IDENTIFY** | make her believe *she* will get this result |
| **DESIRE** | make her want the outcome — emotional, not clinical |
| **ACT** | make the next step feel small and safe |

A section with no job is decoration. A section trying to do all four does none.

## Copy rules

- Write to **her**, in second person. Not about him in third person.
- Lead with the **outcome she wants**, not the procedure name. She does not want an
  abdominoplasty; she wants to stop hiding her stomach.
- **Craft-led copy is a failure mode.** "Surgery, planned in millimetres" is about *him*.
  "You will know exactly what to expect" is about *her*.
- Every claim traceable to `content/facts.md`. Unverified renders as `[[VERIFY]]`, never a
  plausible guess.
- Short sentences. No hedging. No "we strive to."
